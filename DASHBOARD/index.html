01 / MISSION CONTEXT

The orbital debris problem

From object observation to controlled proximity operations.

Uncooperative objects require a coordinated response

Orbital debris can include retired spacecraft, discarded rocket bodies, fragments from breakups and other objects that no longer provide active control. Their motion is governed by orbital dynamics, while their physical attitude may be unknown, irregular or tumbling.

A capture servicer therefore cannot rely on visual proximity alone. It must maintain an estimate of relative position and velocity, evaluate approach geometry, monitor uncertainty and inhibit capture when the selected scenario does not satisfy its configured safety conditions.

ADCS explores this sequence as an integrated concept: orbital-object screening feeds target assessment; target assessment informs rendezvous and approach planning; and verified approach conditions govern the selection and demonstration of a capture mechanism.

01

Dynamic orbital motion

Objects continue moving along their orbital paths. Relative motion must be estimated over time rather than treated as a stationary-target problem.

02

Uncertain target attitude

An object may rotate or tumble. Its orientation must be inferred from observations or represented as an uncertain state in the demonstrator.

03

Controlled capture

Gripper and net deployment are separate physical sequences. The mechanism must move around or toward the target; the target should not simply move itself into the mechanism.

04

Safe decision gates

Capture, hold and abort are distinct mission cases. A selected capture case does not override an unsatisfied safety gate.

05 / CAPTURE CONFIGURATION

Three-arm capture architecture

Capture mode is selected according to the estimated target geometry and the limits of the demonstrator.

Mechanism A Side arms

Cooperative gripper capture

Two independently articulated side arms each carry a two-finger gripper. For a suitable target, the arms coordinate their approach and contact sequence to establish a controlled grasp. The exact sequence depends on the target geometry, relative motion and available contact conditions.

  1. 01 Estimate target position, relative velocity and observable attitude.
  2. 02 Plan a compatible approach and align the end effectors.
  3. 03 Coordinate contact and close the grippers under configured limits.
  4. 04 Verify grasp stability before beginning transfer.
Mechanism B Central arm

Circular ring-net capture

A central articulated arm carries a controlled circular ring-net deployment mechanism. The ring and net are deployed around the predicted target envelope, accounting for the target's motion and rotation. The target is not intentionally propelled into the net as a substitute for net deployment.

  1. 01 Estimate the target trajectory, tumble state and uncertainty.
  2. 02 Move the central arm toward a planned capture geometry.
  3. 03 Deploy the ring and net so the net surrounds the moving target.
  4. 04 Represent tether closure, containment verification and controlled retraction.
  5. 05 Transfer the contained object through the fixed funnel and one-way gate.

09 / TECHNICAL BOUNDARIES

Data integrity and model limits

BOUNDARY A

Orbital state is not physical attitude

Catalogue-derived orbital data can support position and velocity propagation within its limitations. It does not, by itself, establish a debris object's exact yaw, pitch, roll, spin axis or angular rate. Those quantities require observations, an attitude-estimation method or explicit simulation assumptions.

BOUNDARY B

Screening is not capture clearance

A candidate falling within a configured screening radius is not automatically safe to approach or capture. Relative speed, uncertainty, target motion, geometry, mechanism limits and the defined decision gates must be assessed separately.

BOUNDARY C

A 3D view is not proof of orbital accuracy

Visual orbital motion can demonstrate a concept, but validated trajectory claims require defined reference frames, time systems, propagation models, numerical integration or propagation settings, and documented verification tests.

BOUNDARY D

Capture visualization is not hardware control

The web simulation illustrates a proposed capture sequence. It does not directly authorize or command a physical robot. A real prototype requires a separate interface, actuator limits, collision checks, sensor feedback and appropriate hardware safety interlocks.
