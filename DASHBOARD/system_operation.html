MISSION DOSSIER · ADCS-01

Autonomous Space Debris Monitoring and Capture System

A conceptual autonomous orbital-servicing system for detecting, assessing, approaching, capturing, containing, and managing selected space debris in low Earth orbit. This dossier describes the proposed mission workflow, sensing and guidance architecture, capture mechanisms, and onboard decision sequence.

01 / Mission definition

From orbital uncertainty to controlled action

The system combines orbital data, onboard observations, relative-motion estimation, target assessment, and a capture-and-containment sequence within one coordinated workflow.

The challenge

Uncooperative objects are difficult to approach safely

Space debris may have uncertain geometry, incomplete physical information, unknown rotational motion, and limited or no communication capability. A safe rendezvous therefore requires more than identifying an object's approximate orbit.

  • Orbital position and velocity estimates can contain uncertainty.
  • Visual observations may be needed to estimate shape and rotation.
  • Relative motion must be monitored during close approach.
  • Capture selection must account for object geometry and motion.
  • Containment and post-capture stability must be assessed.
The proposed approach

A coordinated autonomous servicing sequence

ADCS is designed as a conceptual framework in which sensing, state estimation, guidance, control, capture selection, and storage management work together to support a simulated debris removal mission.

  • Build and update a representation of the target's motion.
  • Determine whether the target is suitable for an approach.
  • Choose a capture method based on estimated target properties.
  • Coordinate the robotic mechanisms with relative motion.
  • Verify containment before initiating the transfer sequence.
02 / Functional architecture

The mission's decision chain

Each stage produces information or a decision used by the next stage. The implementation is a proposed architecture, not a verified flight-software design.

End-to-end functional flow

A layered model separates data acquisition, estimation, autonomous decisions, physical actions, and mission verification.

Conceptual system flow
STAGE 01

Data acquisition

Orbital catalogues, optical observations, range measurements, and onboard sensors.

STAGE 02

State estimation

Estimate position, velocity, relative range, attitude cues, and angular motion.

STAGE 03

Decision layer

Assess risk, target suitability, approach conditions, and capture options.

STAGE 04

Guidance and action

Generate approach commands and coordinate spacecraft and robotic mechanisms.

STAGE 05

Verification

Check capture status, target motion, containment, and mission-state transitions.

Feedback principle: The process is not purely linear. New measurements and mechanism feedback update the state estimate and can cause the controller to hold, retry, retreat, or abort an operation.

04 / Capture subsystem

Three coordinated arms. Three capture roles.

The selected concept uses two cooperative side arms with two-finger grippers and one central arm carrying a controlled circular ring-net deployment mechanism.

CAPTURE MODE 01

Single-side-arm grasp

Small, accessible targets

One six-degree-of-freedom side manipulator approaches a suitable grasp region and closes its two-finger gripper after alignment and relative-motion checks.

  • Estimate a suitable grasp location.
  • Align the gripper with the target.
  • Close and verify the grasp.
  • Monitor load and target movement.
CAPTURE MODE 02

Cooperative dual-arm grasp

Suitable larger targets

Both side arms coordinate their motion to approach separate suitable grasp regions. The manoeuvre requires coordinated geometry, relative-motion estimates, and load-aware control.

  • Plan compatible grasp locations.
  • Coordinate both six-degree-of-freedom arms.
  • Synchronize contact and grasp confirmation.
  • Check stability before transfer.
CAPTURE MODE 03

Central circular ring-net

Larger or irregular targets

The central six-degree-of-freedom arm positions a controlled circular deployment frame. A net is deployed around a suitable target, with containment checked before retraction and transfer.

  • Approach a safe deployment geometry.
  • Open the ring or deployment frame.
  • Deploy the net around the target.
  • Manage tethers and confirm containment.
Capture decision: The mechanism is selected from estimated object size, shape, accessible grasp regions, motion, uncertainty, and the capability limits of the servicer. The labels “small,” “larger,” and “irregular” are qualitative categories; exact selection thresholds must be defined and validated for the simulation and physical prototype.
Central arm / ring-net sequence

From deployment to secure containment

The following sequence represents the proposed ring-net operation. The actual mechanism timing, tether behaviour, and contact dynamics must be implemented and tested separately.

01 · Stowed Net and deployment frame remain secured during approach.
02 · Position Central arm aligns the mechanism with the selected target.
03 · Deploy Ring frame opens and the net is released around the target.
04 · Contain Net and tether behaviour are checked for successful enclosure.
05 · Transfer Arm retracts and guides the contained object toward the inlet.
05 / Post-capture handling

A fixed, controlled storage path

Capture is not considered complete merely because a target is contacted. The object must be checked, transferred, and retained within the proposed containment system.

01

Capture confirmation

Check grasp, net enclosure, residual motion, and mechanism status.

02

Arm retraction

Move the captured object toward the transfer region under controlled motion.

03

Funnel alignment

Guide the object toward a fixed inlet and alignment collar.

04

Controlled insertion

A pusher advances the object through the inlet when conditions permit.

05

Retention check

A one-way gate and storage-state checks support object retention.

Storage arrangement: The canister remains fixed to the spacecraft structure. The concept uses a guided funnel, alignment features, a controlled pusher, and a one-way retention gate. The storage design must account for object dimensions, collision risk, cumulative mass, centre-of-mass changes, and available volume.
06 / Navigation and control

Sense. Estimate. Guide. Control.

The GNC concept converts sensor and orbital information into motion estimates, approach trajectories, attitude corrections, and coordinated capture commands.

01 / SENSE

Sensor inputs

Camera, range sensor, inertial measurements, attitude sensors, and orbital data.

02 / ESTIMATE

State estimation

Estimate relative position, relative velocity, range, and observable rotational motion.

03 / GUIDE

Trajectory planning

Generate approach geometry, hold points, safe separation, and retreat options.

04 / CONTROL

Motion correction

Coordinate translational and attitude commands with manipulator motion.

05 / VERIFY

Feedback and checks

Compare observed and expected motion; hold, retry, retreat, or abort if needed.

Guidance

Defines the intended path, relative approach speed, hold points, capture geometry, and available retreat trajectories.

Navigation

Combines observations and motion models to estimate the spacecraft's state and the target's relative state.

Control

Produces attitude and translational corrections while coordinating arm trajectories and monitoring limits.

State information used during approach

Relative position

Estimated target location with respect to the servicing spacecraft.

Relative velocity

Closing rate and direction of motion between the two objects.

Orientation cues

Observable body orientation and rotational motion, with uncertainty.

Confidence

Measurement quality, estimation uncertainty, and data freshness.

08 / Data quality

Separate orbital motion from physical orientation

Reliable mission decisions require clarity about which properties come from orbital data and which must be estimated from observation.

Orbit information

Where is the object going?

Orbital elements or state vectors can support trajectory propagation and predictions of an object's position and velocity, subject to data quality, epoch, and model limitations.

  • Position and velocity at a stated epoch.
  • Predicted orbital motion over a selected interval.
  • Relative geometry between the servicer and target.
  • Data age and propagation uncertainty.
TLE or OMM catalogue data may be propagated with an appropriate orbit model such as SGP4. Predictions are not exact measurements of a target's current state.
Physical orientation

How is the object rotating?

An object's physical attitude and tumble rate are different from its orbital trajectory. These properties generally require suitable observations, a motion-estimation method, and explicit uncertainty handling.

  • Image-based shape and orientation cues.
  • Changes in visible features across observations.
  • Estimated angular motion where observable.
  • Confidence limits and fallback behaviour.
Do not treat orbital elements alone as a direct source of precise debris yaw, pitch, roll, or tumble rate. If the physical attitude is unknown, preserve that uncertainty in the capture decision.
09 / Closed-loop operation

A mission that observes and responds

The control loop links target observations to estimates, decisions, commanded actions, and subsequent verification. Each new observation can change the next action.

Operating principle: The spacecraft should not continue an approach simply because a timer has elapsed. Decisions should depend on estimated state, valid measurements, separation, relative velocity, mechanism state, and defined transition conditions.
10 / Mission-state management

Three operating decision paths

Mission cases determine whether the system attempts a capture, maintains a safe relative configuration, or terminates the current operation.

CASE 01

Capture

Proceed when permitted

The selected target meets the defined readiness checks and the simulation follows the planned approach, alignment, capture, and verification sequence.

  • Valid target-state estimate.
  • Permitted approach conditions.
  • Capture mode and mechanism readiness.
CASE 02

Hold

Maintain and reassess

The system maintains or returns to a defined safe condition while waiting for better observations, acceptable geometry, or completion of required checks.

  • Maintain a controlled separation.
  • Continue monitoring the target.
  • Reassess before resuming the approach.
CASE 03

Abort

Terminate the current attempt

The current capture attempt is stopped when a defined constraint is violated or the system cannot establish sufficient confidence to continue safely.

  • Stop capture-related commands.
  • Enter the defined safe response.
  • Record the reason and current state.
These are conceptual operating modes. Their actual transitions should be implemented through explicit state-machine conditions and validated in simulation. The Hold and Abort responses must not be represented as safe merely because their labels appear on the dashboard.
11 / Validation strategy

What must be demonstrated

A credible demonstration should connect each stated capability to an observable output, test scenario, and defined pass condition.

Test group 01

Detection and tracking

Test target identification, state updates, missing observations, stale data, and changes in target visibility.

Test group 02

Relative motion

Check separation, closing speed, trajectory updates, approach-state transitions, and hold behaviour.

Test group 03

Capture selection

Confirm that the selected mechanism matches the configured target characteristics and capability constraints.

Test group 04

Capture verification

Test successful and unsuccessful grasps, net containment, mechanism feedback, and post-capture state transitions.

Test group 05

Storage and capacity

Verify transfer sequencing, gate state, object retention, stored-object records, and configured mass or volume limits.

Test group 06

Failure response

Introduce stale data, excessive closing rate, failed alignment, invalid sensor readings, and capture timeouts.

Implementation boundary: The presence of a diagram, animation, or dashboard status does not establish that a corresponding algorithm or mechanism is implemented. Record completed, simulated, and planned capabilities separately, and attach test evidence to each implemented function.

Project scope and limitations

ADCS is an academic conceptual project and simulation demonstrator. It is not flight-qualified software, live mission-control infrastructure, a certified collision-avoidance system, or authorization to perform orbital operations. Displayed states, estimates, thresholds, and outcomes may be simulated or illustrative rather than live measurements. Real orbital operations require independently validated models, appropriate mission authorization, safety analysis, and qualified systems.
